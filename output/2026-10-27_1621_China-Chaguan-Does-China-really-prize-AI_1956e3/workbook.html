English Key Points

  • Contrary to claims that China disregards AI safety, it has developed one of the world's most stringent AI governance frameworks.
  • China's updated AI safety framework lists 54 risks and proposes human checkpoints and immediate shutdowns for problematic features.
  • China's concern about loss of control has grown, with the topic appearing over a dozen times in the latest framework versus once in 2024.
  • China views AI as a controllable transformative technology, not an existential threat that requires a coordinated slowdown.
  • Chinese firms are often passive on safety, with only five of ten recently reviewed companies publishing safety evaluations.
  • China lags the US in AI performance by months but is not lagging in risk management.

§5 75 words

“China already has the most stringent AI rules anywhere in the world,” says Kendra Schaefer of Trivium, a consultancy based in Beijing. The foundational document is China’s framework for the governance of AI safety. A second update came out in September. It lists 54 risks under three categories: “inherent” (models behaving in unintended ways), “application” (use by criminals) and “secondary” (social alienation). It also proposes ways to eliminate or, at least, mitigate these threats.

“中国已经拥有世界上最严格的AI规则，”驻北京的咨询公司Trivium的Kendra Schaefer说。基础文件是中国的人工智能安全治理框架。第二次更新于9月发布。它列出了三大类54项风险：“固有”风险（模型以非预期方式行事）、“应用”风险（被犯罪分子利用）和“次生”风险（社会疏离）。它还提出了消除或至少缓解这些威胁的方法。
